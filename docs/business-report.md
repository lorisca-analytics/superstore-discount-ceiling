# Business report: where should we spend the next advertising dollar?

*The 407-word management report as submitted. Graded 100/100.*

Leadership currently reads this business through sales volume, and by that
measure the picture looks healthy. The company turned over $2,297,201 across
9,994 order lines, covering 17 sub-categories and 49 states between 2016 and
2019. Phones leads at $330,007 and Binders is ordered more often than anything
else we sell at 1,316 orders. The natural conclusion is to put the advertising
budget behind the products that already move.

The data does not support that conclusion. Total profit is $286,397, an
overall margin of 12.5%, and sales and profit correlate at only r = 0.42
across sub-categories, a weak positive relationship rather than the tight link
the sales ranking implies. Copiers earns the highest profit in the business,
$55,618 at a 37% margin, on just 68 orders, the lowest order count of anything
we sell. Binders returns only 14.9% margin on a 37.2% average discount. Three
sub-categories lose money outright: Tables at negative $17,725, Bookcases at
negative $3,473 and Supplies at negative $1,189.

The mechanism behind the gap is a discount ceiling. At 20% off or less the
business earns $421,773. Above 20% it loses $135,376, and no discount level
above 20% makes money. Tables shows the pattern per item: $184 profit at full
price, negative $4 at 20% off, negative $63 at 30%, and roughly negative $224
at 40% and above, with 63.6% of Tables line items losing money. The same rule
appears geographically. All 10 loss-making states average above 28% discount,
not one of the 39 profitable states averages above 20%, and across states
with meaningful volume average discount and margin correlate at r = negative
0.98. Texas is third in sales at $170,188 and still loses $25,729 on a 37%
average discount, while California is first at $457,688 and earns $76,381 on a
7% average discount.

The recommendation is **Fund, Fix, Stop.** Fund Paper at 43% margin, Copiers
at 37%, Accessories at 25%, Labels at 44% and Envelopes at 42%, all of them
profitable and none of them scaled yet. Fix pricing first on Binders, Storage
and Chairs, and hold their advertising until discounting comes down. Stop
funding growth in Tables, Bookcases, Machines and Supplies. Capping discounts
at 20% on its own moves profit from $286,397 to $421,773, a gain of $135,376
or 47%, while retaining 86% of orders and 84% of sales. We should point the ad
budget at margin we have not yet scaled rather than at volume we are already
discounting away.
